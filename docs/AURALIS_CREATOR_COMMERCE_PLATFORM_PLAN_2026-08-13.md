## 1. Executive Decision

Auralis Digital should narrow its service direction away from general website design and toward a specific commerce problem:

> **Auralis Digital helps people who already have real products but do not yet have a proper online store launch a complete, professional Shopify operation.**

The near-term service is not a generic web-design agency and is not an AI-consulting offer.

The core customer already has something to sell. Auralis solves the infrastructure gap between:

```text
I make/sell products
        ↓
I take orders through DMs, markets, social media, forms, or manually
        ↓
I need a real online store
        ↓
Auralis builds and launches the Shopify operation
```

The longer-term business model expands from one-time Shopify setup into three compatible revenue layers:

```text
1. Shopify setup revenue
        +
2. Optional recurring store-management revenue
        +
3. Optional Auralis marketplace/distribution margin on sales Auralis generates
```

The seller should retain ownership of their own store and direct sales. Auralis should not default to taking a permanent percentage of every sale a seller generates independently.

Auralis already has relevant operating experience and proof-building assets:

The offer is more specific than general website design and easier for a prospect to understand:

> **You already have the products. Auralis builds the store around them.**

This direction is intended to reduce service sprawl and create a repeatable customer type, sales process, delivery process, and eventual recurring-revenue layer.

### Sell the outcome, not the internal machinery

Nova, OpenClaw, AI-assisted development, and automation are internal leverage unless a specific capability directly matters to the client outcome.

Do not lead Shopify sales messaging with:

- AI;

- OpenClaw;

Lead with the commercial result:

> **A professional Shopify store that lets the seller organize products, take real online orders, and operate a proper ecommerce channel.**

## 3. Ideal Customer Profile

### Primary customer

A person, creator, maker, or small product brand who:

- already has real physical products;
- already posts, displays, or sells those products;
- may already sell at markets, events, pop-ups, festivals, or through social media;
- may currently take orders through Facebook, Instagram DMs, TikTok, text, email, forms, Venmo/Cash App, or other manual processes;
- has no proper ecommerce storefront, or has an incomplete/unprofessional store;
- has enough product readiness and business seriousness to justify Shopify;
- can provide product information, pricing, images, inventory/fulfillment expectations, and required business details;
- is willing to own and operate a real Shopify store after launch.

### Strong prospect categories

Potential categories include:

- handmade jewelry;
- crystals and wire wraps;
- candles;
- soaps and bath/body products;
- clothing and streetwear;
- festival/EDM products;
- art and prints;
- crochet and textile goods;
- tumblers;
- custom signs;
- woodworking;
- 3D printed products;
- accessories;
- pet products;
- packaged products suitable for online sale;
- other maker/creator brands with a real catalog.

### Strong buying signals

High-value signals include:

- `DM to order`;
- regular product posts;
- customer comments asking price/availability;
- event/vendor participation;
- recurring product launches;
- clear brand identity;
- several products or variants;
- no ecommerce checkout;
- a Linktree/social profile that does not lead to a complete store;
- manual order instructions;
- visible effort being put into the product business.

### Poor-fit leads

Avoid spending significant time on:

- idea-stage creators with no real products;
- inactive accounts;
- people with one experimental product and no evidence of selling;
- established sellers who already have a strong, functioning store and do not need migration/optimization;
- sellers whose products create legal, platform, rights, safety, fulfillment, or payment-processing problems Auralis is not prepared to support;
- businesses that primarily need a normal informational website rather than ecommerce.

### What counts as a qualified lead

A lead should count toward the 50-lead validation target only when Auralis has reasonably verified all of the following:

1. **Real products** — there is an actual catalog or repeated product offering.
2. **Recent selling behavior** — active posts, markets/events, inquiries, launches, or other evidence of commerce.
3. **Meaningful store gap** — no complete store, manual ordering, or a sufficiently weak/incomplete ecommerce setup.
4. **Viable contact route** — public business email, business DM path, contact form, or another appropriate channel.
5. **Plausible ability to buy the service** — enough business seriousness that a paid setup is credible.
6. **No obvious disqualifying issue** — no known product, rights, safety, fulfillment, or platform problem Auralis is not prepared to support.

Random accounts and low-fit contacts do not count simply because outreach was sent.

## 4. Client Readiness Gate

Before quoting or accepting a project, assess whether the prospect is actually ready for Shopify.

### Readiness inputs

The seller should be able to provide or make decisions about:

- real product list;
- approved prices;
- product variants/options;
- usable product images;
- inventory, made-to-order, POD, or other fulfillment model;
- shipping expectations;
- business/contact information;
- brand assets or an explicit decision to use a minimal starting identity;
- domain status/decision;
- return/refund expectations;
- product weights/dimensions where needed;
- tax/business information they are responsible for providing;
- payment/bank information when required for their Shopify account/payment setup;
- any required product rights/authorization information.

### Readiness result

Classify each serious prospect:

- **PASS** — ready to scope and quote;
- **CONDITIONAL** — can proceed only after named missing inputs are supplied;
- **REJECT / NOT YET** — too early, legally/operationally unsuitable, or too undefined for a controlled project.

Do not turn a bounded Shopify setup into unlimited business consulting because the client has not defined their products, prices, fulfillment, or policies.

## 5. Core Shopify Setup Offer

The service should be sold as a concrete launch outcome, not vague digital consulting.

### Core outcome

> **A complete Shopify store that is organized, professionally presented, tested, and ready for the seller to launch and operate.**

### Potential setup scope

Exact scope should be contract- and package-dependent, but the service can include:

- Shopify store/account configuration;
- theme configuration and layout;
- basic brand implementation using provided assets;
- homepage/storefront structure;
- navigation;
- collections;
- product uploads/imports;
- product descriptions/organization where included;
- product variants and options;
- product images and alt text where included;
- pricing setup using seller-approved data;
- shipping configuration;
- payment configuration guidance/setup within permitted access;
- tax-setting review with clear non-tax-advice boundaries;
- policy setup using seller-approved/business-appropriate information;
- domain connection;
- contact/customer-flow setup;
- mobile QA;
- cart and checkout QA;
- launch checklist;
- basic handoff/training;
- Printify/POD integration when applicable and explicitly scoped.

### Mandatory scope controls before first paid project

The initial package must define, in writing:

- maximum included product count;
- maximum included collection count where applicable;
- included revision rounds;
- whether copywriting is included or only product-data entry/editing;
- whether photography or image editing is excluded;
- whether logo/branding creation is excluded or separately scoped;
- whether domain/email work is included;
- Printify/POD add-on scope;
- migration/import limits;
- custom code/Liquid exclusions or custom-quote path;
- deposit/payment schedule;
- change-order rules;
- handoff/training scope;
- post-launch support window.

A practical first package can be intentionally narrow, for example:

- up to a defined number of products;
- a defined number of core collections;
- seller-provided logo/brand assets;
- standard theme configuration rather than custom-theme engineering;
- one defined revision round;
- launch QA and short post-launch support;
- add-on pricing for additional catalog/copy/migration work.

### Pricing direction

The following values are planning ranges, not locked public pricing:

- **Starter/full setup target:** approximately `$750+` depending on catalog size and complexity.
- **Larger setup target:** approximately `$1,250-$2,000+` where catalog, variants, integrations, migration, or configuration require more work.
- Custom quotes should remain available for larger or unusual scopes.

Treat pricing as a hypothesis until real projects produce data.

After the first several genuine quotes and deliveries, compare:

- quoted price;
- customer reaction;
- close rate;
- actual labor hours;
- scope expansion;
- app/platform costs;
- actual gross margin;
- client satisfaction.

Then revise pricing from evidence rather than theory.

## 6. Shopify Client Store Ownership and Access

Seller ownership is a hard operating principle.

### New Shopify store

Preferred professional workflow:

```text
Auralis / Shopify Partner workflow
        ↓
Create/build the client store using the appropriate Shopify client-transfer/development workflow
        ↓
Configure and QA the scoped store
        ↓
Client selects/activates the required Shopify plan and business/payment setup
        ↓
Transfer store ownership to the client
        ↓
Retain only approved collaborator access if ongoing management is purchased
```

### Existing Shopify store

If the seller already owns the store:

```text
Client remains store owner
        ↓
Auralis requests collaborator access
        ↓
Client grants only permissions required for scoped work
        ↓
Auralis performs project
        ↓
Permissions are retained, reduced, or removed according to handoff/retainer status
```

### Access rules

- Do not rely on shared owner passwords as the normal operating model.
- Use least-privilege access wherever practical.
- The client remains responsible for owner-level identity, business, banking, and tax information.
- Transfer/handoff should be documented.
- Retainer cancellation should include an access-removal/offboarding step.
- Verify the current Shopify Partner/client-transfer/collaborator procedures before each production use because platform requirements can change.

#### Managed Store — approximately `$199-$299/month`

Potential scope:

#### Growth Management — later, approximately `$400-$600+/month`

- deeper analytics;
- seasonal planning;

### Mandatory retainer boundaries

Every plan should state measurable service limits, such as:

- defined product/collection updates per month;
- defined support requests or labor-hours cap;

### Retainer principle

A monthly fee should pay Auralis for defined ongoing labor, management, analysis, and support. It should not be disguised as a percentage of the seller's independent revenue.

## 9. Long-Term Revenue: Auralis Marketplace / Distribution

The higher-upside direction is to let selected independent product brands also sell through an Auralis multi-brand storefront.

The initial goal is **not** to build an Etsy clone or a complex custom multi-vendor platform.

The preferred staged model is:

```text
Seller owns their own Shopify business
        ↓
Auralis optionally features/distributes selected seller products
        ↓
Auralis generates a marketplace sale
        ↓
Seller fulfills the product
        ↓
Auralis earns a retailer/distribution margin
```

### Core fairness rule

If the seller generates a sale through their own store, they keep that direct sale subject to their normal Shopify/payment costs.

If Auralis generates the customer and sale through the Auralis storefront, Auralis may earn an agreed retailer margin.

This is easier to justify than taking a permanent percentage of all seller revenue.

### Retailer-margin wording

Where Shopify Collective or another retailer/supplier model is used, describe the economics as **retailer margin**, not as an automatic commission on all seller revenue.

Illustrative structure:

```text
Retail price: $50
Supplier/wholesale cost to Auralis: $40
Auralis gross retailer margin: $10 (20%)
```

The exact economics must be negotiated and modeled per supplier/product.

### Planning margin direction

A possible starting target is approximately **15%-30% retailer margin on Auralis-generated marketplace sales**, subject to:

- product economics;
- seller gross margin;
- shipping economics;
- returns/refunds;
- payment/platform costs;
- customer acquisition cost;
- taxes;
- support burden;
- negotiated supplier terms.

This percentage is not a locked standard and should not be promised before seller economics are modeled.

## 10. Shopify Collective as Preferred Marketplace Infrastructure to Evaluate

Shopify Collective is the preferred platform to evaluate before custom marketplace engineering.

The relevant operating model is:

- suppliers share selected products through Collective price lists;
- Auralis acts as the retailer;
- selected supplier products are imported into the Auralis store;
- inventory/product data can stay synchronized within Collective-supported behavior;
- when an Auralis customer buys a supplier product, the order is routed to the supplier;
- the supplier fulfills directly to the customer;
- Shopify Collective handles supported retailer/supplier payment routing;
- Auralis earns the retailer margin defined by the supplier-retailer economics.

This allows Auralis to test multi-brand retail without buying large amounts of inventory or building a full custom seller platform.

### Collective eligibility gate

Do not assume every Auralis client can automatically become a Collective supplier.

Before any Collective pilot or supplier onboarding, verify the then-current official Shopify requirements for both Auralis and the supplier, including as applicable:

- eligible active Shopify plans;
- supported country/region relationship;
- compatible store currency requirements;
- Shopify Payments eligibility/configuration;
- payout/payment requirements;
- retailer/supplier account eligibility;
- live-store requirements versus Partner development/client-transfer environments;
- product/category restrictions;
- shipping setup compatibility;
- tax/accounting treatment;
- supplier price-list and margin configuration;
- any additional Collective restrictions current at the time of onboarding.

A seller who is a strong Shopify setup client is **not automatically** a valid marketplace/Collective supplier.

### Important marketplace distinction

Auralis should initially be a **curated retailer/distribution channel**, not an open marketplace where anyone can create an account and self-publish products.

Seller onboarding should be selective.

## 11. Marketplace Rollout Phases

### Phase A — Shopify setup service only

Goal: prove that product sellers will pay Auralis to build their Shopify stores.

Success gate:

- real qualified outreach;
- first paid Shopify setup client;
- successful delivery;
- acceptable project profitability;
- testimonial/case study where permission exists.

No marketplace engineering is required.

### Phase B — Featured Makers / Brand Directory

Create a simple curated Auralis discovery surface.

A seller page can contain:

- maker/brand name;
- short story;
- product/category preview;
- approved images;
- link to the seller's own Shopify store.

Auralis does not process the seller's transaction in this phase.

Purpose:

- test whether Auralis can generate outbound shopping traffic;
- give setup clients optional visibility;
- learn which categories attract engagement;
- create marketplace content before assuming a multi-vendor checkout is needed.

### Attribution requirement

Featured Makers must be instrumented so distribution value can be measured.

Use one or more appropriate attribution methods, such as:

- UTM-tagged outbound links;
- seller-specific referral URLs;
- seller-specific discount/referral codes where appropriate;
- tracked outbound clicks;
- seller-confirmed attributed purchases where measurable.

Do not advance because the directory looks professional. Advance because Auralis can demonstrate meaningful consumer traffic, shopping intent, or attributable sales for participating sellers.

### Phase B evidence gate

Before moving into retailer/Collective activity, look for evidence such as:

- measurable seller-page traffic;
- outbound store clicks;
- repeated consumer engagement with multi-brand browsing;
- attributed purchases where measurable;
- sellers wanting continued placement;
- evidence that Auralis adds distribution value rather than only providing another profile page.

### Phase C — Small Shopify Collective pilot

Pilot with approximately **2-5 eligible sellers**, not dozens.

Test:

- eligibility verification;
- supplier onboarding;
- product import/sync;
- merchandising;
- checkout;
- order routing;
- fulfillment communication;
- customer support;
- returns/refunds;
- retailer margin;
- supplier experience;
- accounting/tax process;
- real customer demand.

### Phase D — Curated multi-brand Auralis storefront

Only after the pilot works:

Potential structure:

```text
Auralis Shop

|-- Apparel
|-- Jewelry
|-- Art
|-- Home & Decor
|-- Festival / EDM
|-- Accessories
`-- Shop by Maker / Brand
```

Each product should clearly identify the maker/brand.

Lucid Creations can operate as one participating Auralis brand rather than defining the entire marketplace identity.

### Phase E — Seller memberships/promotions, only after traffic exists

Possible later monetization:

- enhanced seller profiles;
- featured placement;
- seasonal promotion packages;
- premium analytics/reporting;
- marketplace campaign inclusion;
- seller tools/support.

## 16. Lead Scoring

Use a simple **100-point Auralis Shopify Lead Score**.

### Product readiness — 25 points

Does the prospect have a real catalog with enough products/variants to justify Shopify?

### Evidence of selling/demand — 20 points

Are there signs of inquiries, repeat selling, events, comments, launches, or active merchandising?

### Store gap — 25 points

Highest score when no real store exists and ordering is manual.

### Business seriousness — 15 points

Brand consistency, active posting, product quality, market attendance, real business identity.

### Contactability — 10 points

Public business email, business DM path, contact form, or other appropriate contact route.

### Auralis fit — 5 points

Does Auralis understand the category and have a credible delivery path?

### Priority bands

- **80-100:** contact now;
- **65-79:** strong secondary lead;
- **50-64:** watch/nurture;
- **below 50:** low priority.

Lead score prioritizes outreach; it does not replace the separate client-readiness gate once the prospect becomes serious.

During validation, interested prospects should transition from AI-assisted research/drafting into **human/founder-led sales**.

Nova/OpenClaw can prepare context, but the founder should handle:

- serious replies;
- discovery conversations;
- needs clarification;
- objection handling;
- scope negotiation;
- pricing/quote discussion;
- close decision;
- relationship-building.

Do not automate away the highest-value part of the process before Auralis has learned why customers buy or refuse.

## 22. Revenue Model Summary

```text

        ↓

        ↓
Seller owns their store
        ↓
Optional Auralis Store Management
(monthly recurring revenue)
```

### Example planning economics

Illustrative only:

- 2 new setup clients/month at `$1,000` average = `$2,000` setup revenue;
- 10 managed-store clients at `$199/month` = `$1,990` recurring management revenue;
- `$10,000/month` Auralis marketplace GMV at `20%` average retailer margin = approximately `$2,000` gross marketplace margin.

Illustrative combined gross business contribution:

```text
$2,000 setup
+ $1,990 retainers
+ $2,000 marketplace margin
= $5,990/month
```

This is **not** a forecast. It is a model showing why the three-layer structure can be more durable than one-time setup work alone.

Real profitability must include:

- labor;
- contractor costs if any;
- Shopify/app/platform fees;
- payment fees;
- refunds/chargebacks;
- marketplace support costs;
- taxes;
- customer acquisition;
- software;
- insurance/legal/accounting needs;
- return/shipping costs borne by Auralis;
