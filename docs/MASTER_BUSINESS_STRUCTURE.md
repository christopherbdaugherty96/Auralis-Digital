## Core Rule

Auralis Digital remains the parent operating business / umbrella for the active commercial and creative assets that belong together.

Do not create a new company, repo, Shopify store, brand surface, or deployment merely because a new idea exists.

Separate something only when separation solves a real ownership, legal, customer, checkout, deployment, product-lifecycle, or operational problem.

## Current Placement Rules

### Auralis small-business service

Belongs under Auralis Digital as the current primary cash engine.

Current scope is narrow: practical small-business websites / digital front doors and optional clearly scoped care / managed-presence support.

It is not a standalone generic agency business.

### Lucid Creations

Remains the owned creative-commerce brand. Shopify remains the commerce backend where useful.

Do not create another store merely to reorganize existing Lucid products.

### Shopify + Printify workbook

Remains an Auralis digital product. The current source already exists inside the Auralis repo and should be finalized there unless a future product platform requires separate deployment.

### Lucid Visuals

Remains a Lucid Creations / Auralis digital product line. It does not need a new company or repo at the current validation stage.

### NovaLIS / OpenClaw

Already has its own technical lifecycle / repository and remains a protected long-horizon technical asset. Its existence does not make it an active Auralis revenue product today.

### Live Music Platform

May remain a distinct Auralis product / subsite with its own implementation repository. It is currently frozen from major expansion during the revenue sprint.

Older materials for website leads, proposals, client onboarding, care plans, demos, technical deployment, and Shopify operations can be reused **only after review against the current lock**.

Do not assume old:

- included services;

- business entities;
- branch relationships;
- client claims

are still current.

## When To Create A New Repo / Store / Brand

Require a concrete reason such as:

- independent software build / release lifecycle;
- separate legal ownership;
- separate customer base and policies;
- separate checkout / payment operations;
- independent domain / deployment with real product need;
- client work that should not live in Auralis source;
- a validated venture whose operations would become materially clearer through separation.

Do not separate merely because a folder has become large.

## Decision Rule

Before promoting or separating a new lane, ask:

1. Is there external demand?
2. Has anyone paid or repeatedly used it?
3. Does it need separate ownership, customers, checkout, policies, deployment, or support?
4. Will separation reduce operational complexity rather than increase it?
5. Is the move consistent with the current Auralis lock?

If the answer is primarily “it is built,” that is not enough.
